## Regions, nets and the lattice

A net over a preordered set $R$ of regions in a C*-algebra $A$ assigns to each region $O$ a star subalgebra $\mathcal A(O)$ of $A$. A C*-algebra is a unital complex C*-algebra, and a star subalgebra is a unital complex star subalgebra. On a lattice, given by an abelian group $G$ of sites, the regions are the subsets of $G$, ordered by inclusion. For sets $O$, $N$ of sites, $O + N = \{x + y : x \in O, y \in N\}$.

**1.** A net over $R$ in a C*-algebra $A$ assigns to each region $O$ a star subalgebra $\mathcal A(O)$ of $A$; an assignment $O \mapsto F(O)$ is a net exactly when it is monotone ($O \le O'$ implies $F(O) \subseteq F(O')$, isotony) and every $F(O)$ is norm-closed.  
<sub>`Sealed.net_iff`</sub>

**2.** The lattice causal index and its translations: for an abelian group $G$, two sets of sites are orthogonal exactly when they are disjoint, and one depends on another exactly when it is contained in it. Translation by $a \in G$ maps a region $O$ to $O + a = \{x : x - a \in O\}$; translations act as automorphisms of the causal index, the product of two automorphisms being their composite and the unit acting as the identity.  
<sub>`Sealed.lattice_index`</sub>

**3.** The axioms of a net $\mathcal A$ over a causal index on $R$. Generation: the norm closure of the star subalgebra generated by all local algebras is $A$. Causality: if $O_1$ and $O_2$ are orthogonal, every element of $\mathcal A(O_1)$ commutes with every element of $\mathcal A(O_2)$. Time slice: if $O$ depends on $O'$, then $\mathcal A(O) \subseteq \mathcal A(O')$. Covariance under a group $\Gamma$ with homomorphisms $\kappa$ into the automorphisms of the causal index and $\alpha$ into the star automorphisms of $A$: $\alpha_\gamma(\mathcal A(O)) = \mathcal A(\kappa_\gamma(O))$ for every $\gamma$ and $O$.  
<sub>`Sealed.net_axioms_iff`</sub>

## The CCR algebra and its states

Let $V$ be a real vector space and $\sigma$ a real bilinear form on $V$. The Hilbert space $\ell^2(V)$ consists of the square-summable complex functions on the set $V$. A bilinear form is alternating when $\sigma(u, u) = 0$ for every $u$. A complex number $z$ satisfies $0 \le z$ when it is real and non-negative.

**4.** The Weyl operators and the CCR algebra: for $u \in V$ the Weyl operator $W(u)$ is the bounded operator on $\ell^2(V)$ with $(W(u) f)(v) = e^{i\sigma(u, v)/2} f(v + u)$; the CCR algebra $\mathrm{CCR}(V, \sigma)$ is the norm closure of the star subalgebra generated by the Weyl operators, a C*-algebra; and $W(u)$, regarded as an element of $\mathrm{CCR}(V, \sigma)$, is the same operator.  
<sub>`Sealed.weyl_and_CCR`</sub>

**5.** For alternating $\sigma$ every Weyl operator is unitary, $W(u)^* = W(-u)$, and the Weyl relation $W(u) W(w) = e^{-i\sigma(u, w)/2} W(u + w)$ holds.  
<sub>`Sealed.weyl_relations`</sub>

**6.** Relabelling. Let $\sigma$ be alternating and $T : V \to V'$ a real linear bijection with $\sigma'(T u, T w) = \sigma(u, w)$. The relabelling isomorphism $\mathrm{CCR}(V, \sigma) \to \mathrm{CCR}(V', \sigma')$ maps $W(u)$ to $W(T u)$ for every $u$, and two star isomorphisms of $\mathrm{CCR}(V, \sigma)$ onto a C*-algebra that agree on every Weyl operator are equal.  
<sub>`Sealed.relabel_weyl`</sub>

**7.** States and their characteristic functions: a state of $\mathrm{CCR}(V, \sigma)$ is a continuous complex linear functional $\omega$; a functional is a state exactly when $\omega(1) = 1$ and $\omega(a^* a) \ge 0$ for every $a$. The characteristic function of a state is $\chi_\omega(u) = \omega(W(u))$.  
<sub>`Sealed.state_iff`</sub>

**8.** Gaussian states and the state condition: a Gaussian state is a state $\omega$ with a symmetric real bilinear form $\gamma$ (the covariance form) and a real linear functional $d$ (the displacement); a state with such $\gamma$ and $d$ is Gaussian exactly when $\chi_\omega(u) = \exp(i\, d(u) - \gamma(u, u)/4)$ for every $u$. A real bilinear form $\gamma$ satisfies the state condition for $\sigma$ exactly when $\gamma(u, u) \ge 0$ and $\sigma(u, w)^2 \le \gamma(u, u)\, \gamma(w, w)$ for all $u$, $w$.  
<sub>`Sealed.gaussianState_iff`</sub>

**9.** Covariance matrices: a phase-space index of $n$ modes is an element of the disjoint union of two copies of $\{1, \dots, n\}$, the positions $q_1, \dots, q_n$ followed by the momenta. The standard symplectic matrix is $\Omega = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ in these blocks, and a real $2n \times 2n$ matrix $\gamma$ is an admissible covariance matrix exactly when it is symmetric and $\gamma - i\Omega$ is positive semidefinite (Hermitian with $\bar x^T (\gamma - i\Omega) x \ge 0$ for every complex vector $x$).  
<sub>`Sealed.admissible_iff`</sub>

## The lattice phase space and symbols

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $G$ an abelian group (the sites) and $\iota$ a finite set (the coordinates of one cell). The group algebra $R[G]$ consists of the finitely supported functions $G \to R$ with the convolution product; the element of $R[G]$ with value $r$ at $a$ and $0$ elsewhere is written $r z^a$, and $R[G]$ is the ring of Laurent polynomials in $s$ variables when $G = \mathbb Z^s$. The lattice phase space is $R[G]^\iota$: a vector $u$ has a value $u(x) \in R^\iota$ at every site $x$, nonzero at finitely many sites. A matrix $M$ over $R[G]$, the symbol, acts on it by matrix multiplication.

**10.** The lattice phase space: its vectors are the $\iota$-tuples of elements of $R[G]$; the value of $u$ at $x$ is the tuple of the coefficients of $u_i$ at $x$; the support of $u$ is the finite set of sites where this value is nonzero; and translation by $a$ is multiplication by $z^a$, which shifts values: $(\tau_a u)(x) = u(x - a)$.  
<sub>`Sealed.lattice_space`</sub>

**11.** Translation-invariant maps and their symbols. An $R$-linear map $L$ of the lattice phase space is translation invariant when it commutes with every translation; this holds exactly when $L$ is $R[G]$-linear. The $R[G]$-linear maps correspond to the $\iota \times \iota$ matrices over $R[G]$, their symbols, by matrix multiplication, and the symbol of a composite is the product of the symbols (thesis (4.13)). In coordinates (thesis (4.8)), with $\ell_m$ the coefficient matrix of $z^m$ in the symbol $M$, $(M u)(x) = \sum_m \ell_m\, u(x - m)$.  
<sub>`Sealed.symbol`</sub>

**12.** Finite range: the range of a symbol $M$ is the finite set of sites $m$ at which some entry of $M$ has a nonzero coefficient, and $\operatorname{supp}(M u) \subseteq \operatorname{supp} u + \operatorname{range} M$ for every vector $u$.  
<sub>`Sealed.symbol_range`</sub>

**13.** Lifted forms. The involution of $R[G]$ maps $r z^a$ to $r z^{-a}$ (for $G = \mathbb Z$, $z \mapsto z^{-1}$); for a matrix $M$ over $R[G]$, $M^{\star T}$ is the transpose of $M$ with the involution applied to every entry. A matrix $B$ over $R$ is regarded as a constant symbol, and it defines the lifted form $\tilde B(u, w) = \sum_{i, j} B_{ij} \sum_x u_i(x)\, w_j(x)$ on the lattice phase space. $B$ is alternating when $v^T B v = 0$ for every $v \in R^\iota$.  
<sub>`Sealed.lifted_form`</sub>

**14.** The twisted criterion (thesis (4.9)–(4.11)): the map of the lattice phase space with symbol $M$ preserves the lifted form of $B$, $\tilde B(M u, M w) = \tilde B(u, w)$ for all $u$, $w$, exactly when $M^{\star T} B M = B$. If moreover $\det B$ is a unit of $R$, then $M$ is invertible with two-sided inverse $B^{-1} M^{\star T} B$. For every $B$, the invertible symbols with $M^{\star T} B M = B$ are closed under products and inverses.  
<sub>`Sealed.twisted_criterion`</sub>

## Reversible and irreversible cellular automata

Let $G$ be an abelian group, $\mathcal A$ a net over the subsets of $G$ in a C*-algebra $A$, and $\alpha$ a homomorphism from $G$ to the star automorphisms of $A$, the translations of the algebra. A unital completely positive map of $A$ is a linear map $T$ with $T(1) = 1$ such that every amplification $T \otimes \mathrm{id}_{M_k}$ is positive. The definition of a reversible QCA does not require the inverse of the rule to be local.

**15.** Reversible quantum cellular automata (thesis Def. 4.1, reversible case): a reversible QCA on $\mathcal A$, covariant under $\alpha$, consists of a star automorphism $T$ of $A$ (the rule) and a finite set $N$ of sites (the neighbourhood); a pair is a reversible QCA exactly when the net is covariant under the translations (acting on regions by $O \mapsto O + a$ and on $A$ by $\alpha$), $T$ commutes with every $\alpha_a$, and $T(\mathcal A(O)) \subseteq \mathcal A(O + N)$ for every region $O$. The composite of two reversible QCA, first $T_1$ then $T_2$, is a reversible QCA with rule $T_2 T_1$ and neighbourhood $N_1 + N_2$ (thesis Cor. 4.3).  
<sub>`Sealed.reversibleQCA_iff`</sub>

**16.** Quantum cellular automata (thesis Def. 4.1) and the two locality notions of thesis §4.3: for a star-ordered C*-algebra $A$, a QCA on $\mathcal A$, covariant under $\alpha$, consists of a completely positive map $T$ of $A$ and a finite set $N$ of sites; a pair is a QCA exactly when $T(1) = 1$, the net is covariant under the translations, $\alpha_a T = T \alpha_a$ for every $a$, and $T(\mathcal A(O)) \subseteq \mathcal A(O + N)$ for every region $O$. A map $T$ of $A$ has finite propagation with neighbourhood $N$ (notion I) when $T(\mathcal A(O)) \subseteq \mathcal A(O + N)$ for every region $O$, and it factorizes with respect to a set $M$ of sites (notion II) when $T(ab) = T(a) T(b)$ for $a \in \mathcal A(O_1)$, $b \in \mathcal A(O_2)$ whenever $O_1 + M$ and $O_2$ are disjoint. Every reversible QCA is a QCA with the same rule and neighbourhood.  
<sub>`Sealed.qca_iff`</sub>

## One mode, nearest neighbour

A chain of single modes has the sites $\mathbb Z$ and one position and one momentum per site, positions first; $\Omega = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ is the one-mode symplectic matrix, also regarded as a constant symbol. A symbol over $\mathbb Z$ is a matrix of Laurent polynomials in $z$, $z^m$ being the element $1 z^m$ of $\mathbb R[\mathbb Z]$; it is symplectic when $M^{\star T} \Omega M = \Omega$, which by the twisted criterion says that its map preserves $\sigma(u, w) = \sum_x u(x)^T \Omega w(x)$.

**17.** One-mode nearest-neighbour rules (thesis Prop. 4.4, corrected). For real $2 \times 2$ matrices $\ell_-$, $\ell_0$, $\ell_+$ the nearest-neighbour symbol $\hat L = \ell_- z^{-1} + \ell_0 + \ell_+ z$ has the entries $(\ell_-)_{ij} z^{-1} + (\ell_0)_{ij} + (\ell_+)_{ij} z$. It is symplectic, $\hat L^{\star T} \Omega \hat L = \Omega$, exactly when one of two cases holds: either $\ell_0 = 0$ and the rule is a pure shift, $\ell_+ = 0$ with $\ell_-^T \Omega \ell_- = \Omega$ or $\ell_- = 0$ with $\ell_+^T \Omega \ell_+ = \Omega$ (the case the thesis omits); or $\ell_0^T \Omega \ell_0 = \Omega$, $\ell_+ = \ell_-$, $\det \ell_+ = 0$ (rank at most one) and $\ell_+^T \Omega \ell_0 + \ell_0^T \Omega \ell_+ = 0$.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_nn_symplectic_iff`</sub>

**18.** Fourier modes (thesis (4.14)–(4.15)). For a symbol $M$ over $\mathbb Z$, the Fourier symbol $\hat M(k)$ at $k \in \mathbb R$ is $M$ evaluated entrywise on the unit circle at $z = e^{-ik}$: $\hat M(k)_{ij} = \sum_m c_m e^{-ikm}$, where $c_m$ is the coefficient of $z^m$ in $M_{ij}$ (a finite sum). If an $n$-mode symbol $M$ is symplectic, $M^{\star T} \Omega M = \Omega$ with $\Omega$ the standard symplectic matrix of $n$ modes, then every Fourier mode is symplectic: $\hat M(k)^\dagger \Omega \hat M(k) = \Omega$ for every real $k$ (thesis p. 82).  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_fourierSymbol`</sub>

**19.** The leapfrog symbol of the lattice Klein–Gordon field. Let $m^2 \in \mathbb R$. The leapfrog symbol is $\hat F = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & z + z^{-1} - m^2 \end{pmatrix}$ and the inverse leapfrog symbol is $\begin{pmatrix} z + z^{-1} - m^2 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$. Both are symplectic and their product (leapfrog times inverse) is $1$. The map $F$ of the leapfrog symbol acts on the values $u(x) = (q(x), p(x))$ of a vector of the one-mode chain by $(F u)(x) = (p(x),\, -q(x) + p(x + 1) + p(x - 1) - m^2 p(x))$, the leapfrog step of the lattice Klein–Gordon field, and the trace of its Fourier symbol is $2 \cos k - m^2$.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_leapfrog_symbol`</sub>

## The lattice CCR net and reversible Gaussian automata

Let $G$ be an abelian group (the sites) and $n$ the number of modes per site. The lattice phase space $V$ is the lattice phase space over $\mathbb R$ whose cell coordinates are the positions and momenta of $n$ modes, positions first; $\Omega$ is the standard symplectic matrix of $n$ modes and $\sigma(u, w) = \sum_x u(x)^T \Omega w(x)$ its lifted form. $e_{x,i}$ is the vector with value the $i$-th unit vector at the site $x$ and $0$ elsewhere, and $\tau_a$ is translation by $a$. For a symbol $M$ (a matrix over $\mathbb R[G]$) we write $L u = M u$ for its map.

**20.** The lattice CCR net. The phase space $V$ is the lattice phase space over $\mathbb R$ with the positions and momenta of $n$ modes as cell coordinates, and $\sigma$ is the lifted form of $\Omega$, which is alternating; the lattice CCR net is built from $V$ with the form $\sigma$, so that it is a net in the CCR algebra $\mathrm{CCR}(V, \sigma)$. The lattice CCR net assigns to a set $O$ of sites the norm closure of the star subalgebra of $\mathrm{CCR}(V, \sigma)$ generated by the Weyl operators $W(u)$ with $\operatorname{supp} u \subseteq O$, and a Weyl operator $W(u)$ lies in $\mathcal A(O)$ exactly when $\operatorname{supp} u \subseteq O$. The translation $\alpha_a$ of the CCR algebra maps $W(u)$ to $W(\tau_a u)$. The net is generating, causal, has the time-slice property ($\mathcal A(O) \subseteq \mathcal A(O')$ whenever $O$ depends on $O'$; for the lattice index this is isotony, which every net has), and is covariant under the translations.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_lattice_ccrNet`</sub>

**21.** Gaussian automata (thesis Def. 4.1, reversible case, and (4.7)). Let $M$ be an $n$-mode symbol over $\mathbb R[G]$ with $M^{\star T} \Omega M = \Omega$ (a symplectic rule). Then there is a reversible QCA on the lattice CCR net, covariant under the translations, whose rule maps $W(u)$ to $W(L u)$ for every $u \in V$ and whose neighbourhood is the range of $M$.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_rule_qca`</sub>

**22.** The local rule (thesis Lemma 4.2 in Gaussian form). Let $M$, $M'$ be $n$-mode symbols over $\mathbb R[G]$ with maps $L$, $L'$, and $e_i = e_{0,i}$ the unit vectors at the site $0$. If $L e_i = L' e_i$ for every $i$, then $M = M'$: a rule is determined by its values on one site. And $M$ is symplectic, $M^{\star T} \Omega M = \Omega$, exactly when these values satisfy the one-cell commutation relations with their translates: $\sigma(L e_i, \tau_x L e_j) = \Omega_{ij}$ if $x = 0$ and $0$ otherwise, for all $i$, $j$ and all $x \in G$.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_local_rule`</sub>

**23.** Shifts and on-site rules (thesis p. 70). For every $a \in G$ the translation $\alpha_a$ is the rule of a reversible QCA with neighbourhood $\{a\}$ (the shift). On the chain $G = \mathbb Z$, every real $2n \times 2n$ matrix $S$ with $S \Omega S^T = \Omega$ gives a reversible QCA with neighbourhood $\{0\}$ whose rule maps $W(u)$ to $W(L_S u)$, where $L_S$ is the map of the constant symbol $S$, $(L_S u)(x) = S u(x)$. For one mode, the rotations $\begin{pmatrix} \cos\varphi & -\sin\varphi \\ \sin\varphi & \cos\varphi \end{pmatrix}$ and the squeezings $\operatorname{diag}(e^r, e^{-r})$ satisfy $S \Omega S^T = \Omega$.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_examples_onsite`</sub>

**24.** Nearest-neighbour examples on the one-mode chain. For real $\varphi$ and $\lambda$, the example system (4.18) of the thesis, the rotation by $\varphi$ with symmetric coupling $\lambda$, has the nearest-neighbour symbol with $\ell_0$ the rotation $\begin{pmatrix} \cos\varphi & -\sin\varphi \\ \sin\varphi & \cos\varphi \end{pmatrix}$ and $\ell_+ = \ell_- = \lambda \begin{pmatrix} \sin\varphi & \cos\varphi \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$; it gives a reversible QCA with neighbourhood $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ whose rule maps $W(u)$ to $W(L u)$. For $m^2 \in \mathbb R$ the leapfrog symbol and the inverse leapfrog symbol likewise give reversible QCA with neighbourhood $\{-1, 0, 1\}$, and their rules are inverse to each other.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_examples_nn`</sub>

## Automorphisms of Weyl type and the leapfrog

Cauchy data of the lattice Klein–Gordon field are pairs $d = (d_0, d_1)$ of finitely supported functions $\mathbb Z \to \mathbb R$; the lattice $\mathbb Z^2$ has points $p = (t, x)$, time first, and a test function is a finitely supported function $\mathbb Z^2 \to \mathbb R$.

**25.** Automorphisms of Weyl type (the content of thesis (4.7), with the character the thesis omits). Let $G$ be an abelian group of sites, $V$ the lattice phase space with $n$ modes per site and $\sigma$ the form lifted from $\Omega$. Let $T$ be a star automorphism of $\mathrm{CCR}(V, \sigma)$ and $\Gamma : V \to V$, $c : V \to \mathbb C$ arbitrary maps with $T(W(u)) = c(u) W(\Gamma u)$ for every $u$. Then $\Gamma$ is a real-linear bijection, it preserves $\sigma$, and $c$ is a character of modulus one: $c(u + w) = c(u) c(w)$ and $|c(u)| = 1$.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_weyl_type`</sub>

**26.** The leapfrog on Cauchy data. Let $m^2 \in \mathbb R$. The Wronskian of Cauchy data is $\sigma_S(d, d') = \sum_x d_0(x) d_1'(x) - \sum_x d_1(x) d_0'(x)$, and the leapfrog $F$ maps $(d_0, d_1)$ to $(d_1, -d_0 + d_1(\cdot + 1) + d_1(\cdot - 1) - m^2 d_1)$. $F$ is a bijection that preserves the Wronskian. There is a real-linear bijection $e$ from Cauchy data onto the one-mode lattice phase space over $\mathbb Z$ with $(e\,d)(x) = (d_0(x), d_1(x))$; it carries the Wronskian to $\sigma$, $\sigma_S(d, d') = \sigma(e\,d, e\,d')$, and $F$ to the map of the leapfrog symbol: $e(F d) = \hat F (e\,d)$.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_cauchy_leapfrog`</sub>

**27.** The lattice Klein–Gordon field and its Cauchy map. Let $m^2 \in \mathbb R$. The discrete Klein–Gordon operator is $(P h)(t, x) = h(t+1, x) + h(t-1, x) - h(t, x+1) - h(t, x-1) + m^2 h(t, x)$ for $h : \mathbb Z^2 \to \mathbb R$. For a test function $f$, the retarded solution $G^+ f$ is the unique $h$ with $P h = f$ that vanishes at all times $t < t_0$ for some $t_0$, the advanced solution $G^- f$ the unique $h$ with $P h = f$ that vanishes at all times $t > t_0$ for some $t_0$, and the causal propagator is $E f = G^- f - G^+ f$, a linear map. The phase space of the lattice field is the quotient of the test functions by the kernel of $E$, and $[f]$ is the class of $f$ in this quotient. The Cauchy map $\Lambda_t$ at time $t$ sends $[f]$ to $((E f)(t, \cdot), (E f)(t + 1, \cdot))$.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_lattice_field`</sub>

**28.** The direction of time (thesis (4.7)). Let $m^2 \in \mathbb R$, $\Lambda_t$ the Cauchy map at time $t$ and $F$ the leapfrog. Then $\Lambda_{t+1} = F \circ \Lambda_t$ for every $t$, and time translation by one step acts on Cauchy data as $F^{-1}$: if $g(p) = f(p - (1, 0))$ for every point $p$ ($g$ is $f$ moved one step to the future), then $F(\Lambda_t [g]) = \Lambda_t [f]$, that is $\Lambda_t [g] = F^{-1} \Lambda_t [f]$, $F$ being bijective.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_time_step`</sub>

## Gaussian states on the lattice

For a state $\omega$ of $\mathrm{CCR}(V, \sigma)$ and a star automorphism $\alpha$, $\omega \circ \alpha$ is the state $x \mapsto \omega(\alpha(x))$. Cells are maps $x \mapsto \gamma_x$ from $\mathbb Z$ to real $2n \times 2n$ matrices; they are summable when every entry is absolutely summable over $x$, symmetric when $\gamma_{-x} = \gamma_x^T$, and of finite range when $\gamma_x = 0$ for $|x| > R$ for some $R$. On the chain $G = \mathbb Z$ the covariance form of cells is $\gamma(u, w) = \sum_{x, y} u(x)^T \gamma_{x-y} w(y)$ and their Fourier series is $\hat\gamma(k) = \sum_x \gamma_x e^{-ikx}$.

**29.** Translation-invariant states. For states of $\mathrm{CCR}(V, \sigma)$: $(\omega \circ \alpha)(x) = \omega(\alpha(x))$; a state is determined by its functional; and a state $\omega$ is translation invariant, $\omega \circ \alpha_a = \omega$ for every $a \in G$, exactly when its characteristic function is, $\chi_\omega(\tau_a u) = \chi_\omega(u)$ for every $a \in G$ and $u \in V$.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_translation_invariant`</sub>

**30.** The action of a rule on Gaussian data. Let $\omega$ be a Gaussian state of $\mathrm{CCR}(V, \sigma)$ with covariance form $\gamma$ and displacement $d$, $M$ an $n$-mode symbol over $\mathbb R[G]$ with map $L$, and $T$ a star automorphism with $T(W(u)) = W(L u)$ for every $u$ (for symplectic $M$ the rule of its automaton is such a $T$). Then $\omega \circ T$ is a Gaussian state with covariance form $\gamma(L \cdot, L \cdot)$ and displacement $d \circ L$.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_gaussian_evolve`</sub>

**31.** Covariance matrices of finite regions (where the state condition on covariance forms meets the admissibility condition $\gamma - i\Omega \ge 0$ on covariance matrices). Let $O$ be a finite set of sites. The site vectors $e_{x,i}$, $x \in O$, are enumerated by a bijection from pairs $(x, i)$ onto the phase-space indices of $n|O|$ modes that sends $(x, q_r)$ to the position index $e(x, r)$ and $(x, p_r)$ to the momentum index $e(x, r)$ of the same mode, for a bijection $e$ from $O \times \{1, \dots, n\}$ onto $\{1, \dots, n|O|\}$; $\gamma_O$ is the matrix of a form $\gamma$ on these vectors, $(\gamma_O)_{(x,i),(y,j)} = \gamma(e_{x,i}, e_{y,j})$, and the covariance matrix of $\gamma$ on $O$ is $\Omega^T \gamma_O \Omega$, with $\Omega$ the standard symplectic matrix of $n|O|$ modes. If $\gamma$ is symmetric, it satisfies the state condition on the vectors supported in $O$ ($\gamma(u, u) \ge 0$ and $\sigma(u, w)^2 \le \gamma(u, u) \gamma(w, w)$ whenever $\operatorname{supp} u, \operatorname{supp} w \subseteq O$) exactly when its covariance matrix on $O$ is admissible. The covariance form of every Gaussian state has an admissible covariance matrix on $O$, and the vacuum form $\sum_x u(x)^T w(x)$ has covariance matrix $1$.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_covariance_matrix`</sub>

**32.** Cells, their covariance form and their Fourier series. For cells $(\gamma_x)$, the covariance form is $\gamma(u, w) = \sum_{i, j} \sum_{x, y} u_i(x) (\gamma_{x-y})_{ij} w_j(y)$ (finite sums over the supports), so that $\gamma(e_{x,i}, e_{y,j}) = (\gamma_{x-y})_{ij}$; it is translation invariant, $\gamma(\tau_a u, \tau_a w) = \gamma(u, w)$, and symmetric when the cells are. For summable cells and every real $k$ the matrix series $\sum_x e^{-ikx} \gamma_x$ converges and $\hat\gamma(k)$ is its sum; $\hat\gamma$ is continuous, and for summable symmetric cells $\hat\gamma(k)$ is Hermitian.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_cells`</sub>

**33.** The Fourier criterion (thesis Lemma 4.5(i)). Let $(\gamma_x)$ be summable symmetric cells with covariance form $\gamma$ and Fourier series $\hat\gamma$. The state condition for $\gamma$ and $\sigma$ is equivalent to $\gamma + i\sigma \ge 0$: for every finite family $u_1, \dots, u_m$ of vectors the complex matrix $(\gamma(u_j, u_l) + i \sigma(u_j, u_l))_{jl}$ is positive semidefinite. If $\hat\gamma(k) + i\Omega$ is positive semidefinite for every real $k$, then $\gamma$ satisfies the state condition; conversely, for cells of finite range, the state condition implies that $\hat\gamma(k) + i\Omega$ is positive semidefinite for every $k$.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_fourier_criterion`</sub>

**34.** Saturation for one mode (the matrix content of thesis Lemma 4.5(ii); purity of states is not part of the statement). Let $(\gamma_x)$ be summable symmetric one-mode cells with $\hat\gamma(k) + i\Omega$ positive semidefinite for every real $k$. Then three conditions are equivalent: $\det \hat\gamma(k) = 1$ for every $k$; $(\Omega \hat\gamma(k))^2 = -1$ for every $k$; and $\hat\gamma(k) + i\Omega$ is singular, $\det(\hat\gamma(k) + i\Omega) = 0$, for every $k$.  
<sub>`Sealed.rev_saturation`</sub>

**35.** The evolution of cells (thesis (4.17), general form). Let $M$ be an $n$-mode symbol over $\mathbb Z$ with map $L$, $\ell_m$ the real coefficient matrix of $z^m$ in $M$, and $(\gamma_x)$ summable cells with covariance form $\gamma$. The cells $\gamma'_x = \sum_{m, m'} \ell_m^T \gamma_{x+m-m'} \ell_{m'}$, the sums running over the range of $M$ (thesis (4.17a)), are summable, symmetric when $(\gamma_x)$ is, and their covariance form is $\gamma(L \cdot, L \cdot)$; their Fourier series is $\hat\gamma'(k) = \hat L(k)^\dagger \hat\gamma(k) \hat L(k)$ for every real $k$, with $\hat L$ the Fourier symbol of $M$.

## Cells and Fourier series on the chain

On the chain $\mathbb Z$ with $n$ modes per site, the symbols are matrices over the group algebra $\mathbb R[\mathbb Z]$ of Laurent polynomials, a vector $u$ of the lattice phase space has a value $u(x) \in \mathbb R^{2n}$ at each site, with coordinates $u_i(x)$ indexed by the phase-space index of $n$ modes (positions first), and $\Omega$ is the standard symplectic matrix. For a Laurent polynomial $p$ write $p_m$ for its coefficient of $z^m$.

**36.** Cells, their forms, Fourier series and pull-backs. Let $\gamma = (\gamma_x)_{x \in \mathbb Z}$ be a family of real $2n \times 2n$ matrices. It is *cell data* exactly when every entry is absolutely summable, $\sum_x |(\gamma_x)_{ij}| < \infty$, and $\gamma_{-x} = \gamma_x^T$ for every $x$. Its form is $\gamma(u, w) = \sum_{i, j} \sum_{x, y} u_i(x)\, (\gamma_{x - y})_{ij}\, w_j(y)$ (a finite sum). Its Fourier series $\hat\gamma(k)$ is the sum of the series $\sum_x e^{-ikx} \gamma_x$ of complex matrices when the series is summable, which it is for cell data, and $0$ by convention otherwise. The on-site cells of a matrix $g_0$ are $g_0$ at the origin and $0$ elsewhere. For a matrix $C$ of Laurent polynomials, of any size, the coefficient matrix $c_m$ of $z^m$ has the entries $(C_{ki})_m$, the range of $C$ is the finite set of $m$ with $c_m \neq 0$, and the pull-back cells of a family $\nu$ along $C$ are $(C_* \nu)_d = \sum_{m, m'} c_m^T\, \nu_{d + m - m'}\, c_{m'}$, the sum over $m$, $m'$ in the range of $C$. For a square matrix $M$ of Laurent polynomials (a symbol) the coefficient matrix $\ell_m$ of $z^m$ likewise has the entries the coefficients of $z^m$, $(\ell_m)_{ij} = (M_{ij})_m$, and the evolved cells are $M_* \gamma$. The Fourier symbol of a square matrix $M$ of Laurent polynomials at the wave number $k$ is $M$ evaluated entrywise at $z = e^{-ik}$, $p \mapsto \sum_m p_m e^{-ikm}$. Finally, the symplectic form $\sigma$ of the lattice phase space $V$, the form from which the lattice CCR net and the CCR algebra $\mathrm{CCR}(V, \sigma)$ are built, is the form lifted from $\Omega$, $\sigma(u, w) = \sum_{i, j} \Omega_{ij} \sum_x u_i(x)\, w_j(x)$.  
<sub>`Sealed.dyn_cells`</sub>

## The example system

From here on the chain carries one mode per site, so that cells and Fourier symbols are real or complex $2 \times 2$ matrices in the coordinates $(q, p)$, written in blocks of $1 \times 1$ matrices, and $\Omega = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$. For real $\varphi$ write $R_\varphi = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\varphi & -\sin\varphi \\ \sin\varphi & \cos\varphi \end{pmatrix}$ for the rotation by $\varphi$ and $\Gamma_\pm = \begin{pmatrix} \sin\varphi & \cos\varphi \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

**37.** The example system (thesis (4.18)). For real $\varphi$ and $\lambda$, the example rule $L_{\varphi, \lambda}$ is the symbol with the coefficient matrices $\ell_0 = R_\varphi$, $\ell_1 = \ell_{-1} = \lambda \Gamma_\pm$ and $\ell_m = 0$ for $|m| \ge 2$. For every wave number $k$, its Fourier symbol is the real matrix $\hat L(k) = R_\varphi + 2\lambda\cos k\,\Gamma_\pm$, and $\hat L(-k) = \hat L(k)$, $\det \hat L(k) = 1$, $\operatorname{tr} \hat L(k) = 2(\cos\varphi + \lambda \cos k \sin\varphi)$ and $\hat L(k)^T \Omega \hat L(k) = \Omega$. A symbol $M$ on the chain with $n$ modes per site is symplectic when $M^{\star T} \Omega M = \Omega$, and every power $L_{\varphi, \lambda}^t$, $t \in \mathbb N$, is symplectic.  
<sub>`Sealed.dyn_example_system`</sub>

**38.** The regime (thesis (4.19)–(4.20)). For real $\varphi$ and $\lambda$, the pair $(\varphi, \lambda)$ lies in the regime exactly when $|\cos\varphi| + |\lambda\sin\varphi| < 1$ and $\lambda\sin\varphi \neq 0$. The first condition holds exactly when every mode is elliptic, $|\operatorname{tr}\hat L(k)| < 2$ for every real $k$, and it implies $\sin\varphi \neq 0$. For $\sin\varphi \neq 0$ it is equivalent to $|\lambda| < f_{\rm crit}$ with $f_{\rm crit} = (1 - |\cos\varphi|)/|\sin\varphi|$ (4.20), and $f_{\rm crit} = \tan(\varphi/2)$ for $0 < \varphi \le \pi/2$, $f_{\rm crit} = \cot(\varphi/2) = 1/\tan(\varphi/2)$ for $\pi/2 < \varphi < \pi$.  
<sub>`Sealed.dyn_regime`</sub>

**39.** The rotation angle, the powers of the symbol and the spectral projectors (thesis (4.19), Lemma 4.6 and (4.21)–(4.24)). For real $\varphi$, $\lambda$ and $k$ let $\theta(k) = \arccos(\cos\varphi + \lambda\cos k\sin\varphi)$, with the real arccosine, which takes values in $[0, \pi]$ and is extended by $0$ above $1$ and by $\pi$ below $-1$; let $N(k) = (\sin\theta(k))^{-1}(\cos\theta(k)\, 1 - \hat L(k))$, a real matrix, with the convention $0^{-1} = 0$ (in the regime $\sin\theta(k) > 0$), and let $P_k = \frac12(1 + i N(k))$, a complex matrix with complex conjugate $\bar P_k$. The angle $\theta$ is even and continuous. In the regime: on $[0, \pi]$, $\theta$ is strictly increasing if $\lambda\sin\varphi > 0$ and strictly decreasing if $\lambda\sin\varphi < 0$; and for every $k$, $\cos\theta(k) = \cos\varphi + \lambda\cos k\sin\varphi$, $\operatorname{tr}\hat L(k) = 2\cos\theta(k)$, $0 < \theta(k) < \pi$, $\theta$ is differentiable at $k$ with $\theta'(k) = \lambda\sin\varphi\sin k/\sin\theta(k)$, $\hat L(k)^t = \frac{\sin t\theta(k)}{\sin\theta(k)}\, \hat L(k) - \frac{\sin (t - 1)\theta(k)}{\sin\theta(k)}\, 1$ and $\hat L(k)^t = \cos t\theta(k)\, 1 - \sin t\theta(k)\, N(k)$ for every $t \in \mathbb N$, $N(k)^2 = -1$, $P_k + \bar P_k = 1$, $P_k^2 = P_k$, $P_k \bar P_k = \bar P_k P_k = 0$, $\hat L(k) = e^{i\theta(k)} P_k + e^{-i\theta(k)} \bar P_k$, and $\hat L(k)^t = e^{it\theta(k)} P_k + e^{-it\theta(k)} \bar P_k$ for every $t \in \mathbb N$.  
<sub>`Sealed.dyn_angle_projectors`</sub>

**40.** The invariant form (thesis (4.28)). For real $\varphi$, $\lambda$ and $k$ let $\hat\varepsilon(k) = -\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(\sin\varphi)}{\sin\theta(k)}\, \Omega\,(\cos\theta(k)\, 1 - \hat L(k))$, with $\operatorname{sgn}$ the sign function ($\operatorname{sgn} 0 = 0$) and the convention $1/0 = 0$ for the quotient (in the regime $\sin\theta(k) > 0$). For all parameters, $\hat\varepsilon(-k) = \hat\varepsilon(k)$ and $\hat L(k)^T \hat\varepsilon(k) \hat L(k) = \hat\varepsilon(k)$. In the regime, $\hat\varepsilon$ is a continuous function of $k$, and for every $k$: $\hat\varepsilon(k) = \frac{\operatorname{sgn}(\sin\varphi)}{\sin\theta(k)} \begin{pmatrix} \sin\varphi & -\lambda\cos k\sin\varphi \\ -\lambda\cos k\sin\varphi & \sin\varphi - 2\lambda\cos k\cos\varphi \end{pmatrix}$; $\hat\varepsilon(k)$ is symmetric and positive definite, $\det\hat\varepsilon(k) = 1$ and $(\Omega\hat\varepsilon(k))^2 = -1$; $\hat\varepsilon(k) = \operatorname{sgn}(\sin\varphi)\, i\Omega\,(P_k - \bar P_k)$; and for every real $g$, the complex matrix $g\hat\varepsilon(k) + i\Omega$ is positive semidefinite exactly when $g \ge 1$.  
<sub>`Sealed.dyn_invariant_form`</sub>

**41.** The stationary covariance cells of the example system (thesis Theorem 4.8, with the completeness direction, which goes beyond the thesis). Let $(\varphi, \lambda)$ lie in the regime and let $(\gamma_x)$ be cell data on the chain with one mode per site that are reflection symmetric, $\gamma_{-x} = \gamma_x$ for every $x$. Then the following are equivalent: (i) the cells are stationary, $(L_{\varphi, \lambda})_* \gamma = \gamma$, and satisfy the Fourier state condition, $\hat\gamma(k) + i\Omega \ge 0$ (positive semidefinite) for every real $k$; (ii) there is a continuous function $g : \mathbb R \to \mathbb R$ with $g(-k) = g(k)$ and $g(k) \ge 1$ for every $k$ such that $\hat\gamma(k) = g(k)\, \hat\varepsilon(k)$ for every $k$.  
<sub>`Sealed.dyn_stationary_iff`</sub>

**42.** The lattice Klein–Gordon leapfrog and its inverse have a mode that is not elliptic, for every real $m^2$; for $m^2 = 0$ the inverse is the example rule at $(\pi/2, 1)$, on the boundary of the regime. For real $m^2$ let $F$ be the leapfrog symbol of the lattice Klein–Gordon field with mass squared $m^2$, and $F^{-1}$ the symbol with $F F^{-1} = 1$. At every wave number $k$ the Fourier symbols of $F$ and $F^{-1}$ have the trace $2\cos k - m^2$, and for each of them some mode is not elliptic: there is a $k$ with $|\operatorname{tr}\hat F(k)| \ge 2$, and one with $|\operatorname{tr}\widehat{F^{-1}}(k)| \ge 2$. For $m^2 = 0$, $F^{-1}$ is the example rule $L_{\pi/2, 1}$, and $(\pi/2, 1)$ is not in the regime, lying on its boundary: $|\cos\frac\pi2| + |1 \cdot \sin\frac\pi2| = 1$.

## Convergence

The Fourier conventions are those of the section on Gaussian states on the lattice, $\hat\gamma(k) = \sum_x e^{-ikx}\gamma_x$. Integrals over $[-\pi, \pi]$ are Lebesgue integrals for the Lebesgue measure, and the limit of a sequence $(a_t)_{t \in \mathbb N}$ is taken as $t \to \infty$.

**43.** The Riemann–Lebesgue lemma along a phase. Let $s \subseteq \mathbb R$ be measurable and let $\theta : \mathbb R \to \mathbb R$ be injective on $s$ and, at every $x \in s$, differentiable within $s$ with derivative $\theta'(x) \neq 0$. Then for every $h : \mathbb R \to \mathbb C$, $\int_s h(k)\, e^{ir\theta(k)}\, dk \to 0$ as the real number $r$ tends to $\infty$. The integral is the Lebesgue integral, which takes the value $0$ for a non-integrable integrand, so that the statement has content when $h$ is integrable on $s$.  
<sub>`Sealed.dyn_riemann_lebesgue`</sub>

**44.** The limit covariance (thesis Prop. 4.7). For real $\varphi$, $\lambda$ and a family $(\gamma_x)$ of real $2 \times 2$ matrices, the limit matrix is $C(k) = \frac12\bigl(\hat\gamma(k) + N(k)^T \hat\gamma(k) N(k)\bigr)$, and the limit cells are the real parts $\gamma_{\infty, x} = \operatorname{Re} \frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} e^{ikx}\, C(k)\, dk$, entrywise. Let $(\varphi, \lambda)$ lie in the regime. For every $k$, $C(k)$ is invariant, $\hat L(k)^T C(k) \hat L(k) = C(k)$, and if $\hat\gamma(k) + i\Omega$ is positive semidefinite, so is $C(k) + i\Omega$. If $(\gamma_x)$ are cell data, then every entry of $k \mapsto e^{ikx} C(k)$ is integrable on $[-\pi, \pi]$, the integral $\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} e^{ikx}\, C(k)\, dk$ is real and hence equal to $\gamma_{\infty, x}$, and $\gamma_{\infty, -x} = \gamma_{\infty, x}^T$ for every $x$; if moreover the cells are reflection symmetric, $\gamma_{-x} = \gamma_x$, and $\hat\gamma(k) + i\Omega \ge 0$ at the wave number $k$, then $C(k) = g\, \hat\varepsilon(k)$ for a real number $g \ge 1$ (the form of thesis Theorem 4.8 at this wave number).  
<sub>`Sealed.dyn_limit_covariance`</sub>

**45.** Convergence of the covariance (thesis Prop. 4.7). Let $(\gamma_x)$ be cell data on the chain with one mode per site and $\gamma^{(t)} = (L_{\varphi, \lambda*})^t \gamma$ the cells evolved $t$ times. For all real $\varphi$, $\lambda$, $k$ and $t \in \mathbb N$, $\hat\gamma^{(t)}(k) = (\hat L(k)^t)^T\, \hat\gamma(k)\, \hat L(k)^t$. If $(\varphi, \lambda)$ lies in the regime, then for every site $x$ and every entry, $(\gamma^{(t)}_x)_{ab}$ converges to the corresponding entry of the limit cell $\gamma_{\infty, x}$, and for all vectors $u$, $w$ of the lattice phase space the forms converge, $\gamma^{(t)}(u, w) \to \gamma_\infty(u, w)$, where $\gamma_\infty$ is the form of the limit cells.  
<sub>`Sealed.dyn_tendsto_cells`</sub>

**46.** Without coupling the covariance need not converge. There are reflection-symmetric cell data $(\gamma_x)$ on the chain with one mode per site satisfying the Fourier state condition, $\hat\gamma(k) + i\Omega \ge 0$ for every $k$, such that for the uncoupled example rule $L_{\pi/2, 0}$ (a quarter turn at every site, $\lambda = 0$) the position-position entry of the evolved cell at the origin, $(\gamma^{(t)}_0)_{qq}$ with $\gamma^{(t)} = (L_{\pi/2, 0*})^t\gamma$, does not converge as $t \to \infty$.  
<sub>`Sealed.dyn_no_coupling`</sub>

**47.** Convergence of the states (the weak-* part of thesis Theorem 4.9). Let $(\varphi, \lambda)$ lie in the regime, let $(\gamma_x)$ be cell data on the chain with one mode per site, and let $\omega$ be a Gaussian state of the CCR algebra of the lattice phase space with covariance form the form of $(\gamma_x)$ and displacement $0$. For $t \in \mathbb N$ let $\alpha_t$ be the star automorphism of the CCR algebra with $\alpha_t(W(u)) = W(L_{\varphi, \lambda}^t u)$ (the automorphism of the symplectic rule $L^t_{\varphi, \lambda}$), and $\omega_t = \omega \circ \alpha_t$ the evolved Gaussian state. Then the characteristic functions converge pointwise, $\omega_t(W(u)) \to \exp(-\gamma_\infty(u, u)/4)$ for every $u$, with $\gamma_\infty$ the form of the limit cells, and there is a Gaussian state $\omega_\infty$ with covariance form $\gamma_\infty$ and displacement $0$ such that $\omega_t(a) \to \omega_\infty(a)$ for every element $a$ of the CCR algebra. (The symplecticity of the powers, supplied as a hypothesis, always holds.)

## Gaussian channels and irreversible automata on the chain

For real bilinear forms $\beta$, $\beta'$ on a real vector space $V$, the condition $\beta + i\beta' \ge 0$ means that for every finite family $u_1, \dots, u_m$ in $V$ the complex matrix $(\beta(u_j, u_k) + i\beta'(u_j, u_k))_{jk}$ is positive semidefinite. A completely positive map of a C*-algebra is a linear map all of whose matrix amplifications are positive. On the chain, $\tau_a$ is the translation of the lattice phase space by $a \in \mathbb Z$, the lattice CCR net assigns to $O \subseteq \mathbb Z$ the local algebra $\mathcal A(O)$, and the translations act on the CCR algebra by the star automorphisms $\alpha_a$ with $\alpha_a(W(u)) = W(\tau_a u)$. The range $N_M$ of a symbol $M$ is the set of $m$ at which some entry has a nonzero coefficient. Statements about lattice channels concern the channels with the given data; the existence of a Gaussian channel with prescribed data is not asserted.

**48.** Gaussian channels (thesis §4.3). Let $\sigma$ be a real bilinear form on a real vector space $V$. A Gaussian channel on $\mathrm{CCR}(V, \sigma)$ consists of a linear map $L : V \to V$, a bilinear form $\nu$ (the noise), a linear form $\mu$ (the displacement) and a completely positive map $f$ of the CCR algebra; such a quadruple is a Gaussian channel exactly when $\nu$ is symmetric, the channel condition (4.34) $\nu + i(\sigma - \sigma(L\cdot, L\cdot)) \ge 0$ holds, and $f(W(u)) = \exp(i\mu(u) - \nu(u, u)/4)\, W(Lu)$ for every $u \in V$. For bilinear forms $\beta$, $\beta'$ on $V$, the condition $\beta + i\beta' \ge 0$ is the condition on Gram matrices stated at the head of this section. The concatenation $T_2 \circ T_1$ of two Gaussian channels has the map $a \mapsto f_2(f_1(a))$, the linear map $L_2 L_1$, the noise $\nu_1 + \nu_2(L_1\cdot, L_1\cdot)$ and the displacement $\mu_1 + \mu_2 \circ L_1$.  
<sub>`Sealed.dyn_gaussian_channel`</sub>

**49.** Lattice channels (thesis p. 92) are quantum cellular automata. Let $T$ be a Gaussian channel on the CCR algebra of the lattice phase space of the chain with $n$ modes per site, $M$ a symbol and $(\nu_x)$ a family of real $2n \times 2n$ matrices. $T$ is a lattice channel with rule $M$ and noise cells $(\nu_x)$ exactly when its linear map is $u \mapsto Mu$, its noise is the form of $(\nu_x)$, the family $(\nu_x)$ is cell data, and its displacement is translation invariant, $\mu(\tau_a u) = \mu(u)$ for all $a$ and $u$. A lattice channel commutes with the translations, $\alpha_a \circ T = T \circ \alpha_a$, and there is a quantum cellular automaton on the lattice CCR net, covariant under the translations, with rule $T$ and neighbourhood the range $N_M$.  
<sub>`Sealed.dyn_lattice_channel`</sub>

**50.** Locality of lattice channels (thesis Lemma 4.11, in both directions). Let $M$ be a symbol on the chain with $n$ modes per site. The pull-back cells of the on-site cells $\delta_{\cdot,0}\Omega$ along $M$ are the coefficient matrices of $M^{\star T}\Omega M$, and their form is $\sigma(M\cdot, M\cdot)$. Let $T$ be a lattice channel with rule $M$ and noise cells $(\nu_x)$. (I) For every set $N \subseteq \mathbb Z$, $T$ has finite propagation with neighbourhood $N$ exactly when $N_M \subseteq N$. (II) For every finite set $M' \subseteq \mathbb Z$ with $0 \in M'$ and $-x \in M'$ for every $x \in M'$, $T$ factorizes with respect to $M'$ exactly when $\nu_x = 0$ for every $x \notin M'$ and the range of $M^{\star T}\Omega M$ is contained in $M'$ (the cells of $\sigma(M\cdot, M\cdot)$ vanish outside $M'$; thesis (4.36)).  
<sub>`Sealed.dyn_locality`</sub>

**51.** Factorization does not imply finite propagation, and the converse separation for the rule $0$ holds for every lattice channel with the given data (thesis Lemma 4.10(v)); the existence of such a channel is not proved. Let $n \ge 1$ be the number of modes per site. (a) There is a Gaussian channel that is a lattice channel with the shift as rule (the symbol $z \cdot 1$) and noise cells $0$, that factorizes with respect to $\{0\}$, and that does not have finite propagation with neighbourhood $\{0\}$. (b) For every finite set $M' \subseteq \mathbb Z$ there are cell data $(\nu_x)$ of finite range, $\nu_m = 0$ for $|m| > R$ for some $R \in \mathbb N$, such that the rule $0$ and the noise $\nu$ satisfy the channel condition $\nu + i(\sigma - \sigma(0\cdot, 0\cdot)) \ge 0$, and no lattice channel with rule $0$ and noise cells $(\nu_x)$ factorizes with respect to $M'$. (That such a channel exists rests on the characterization of Gaussian channels by their data, thesis Thm. 2.6, which is not asserted here.)  
<sub>`Sealed.dyn_independence`</sub>

**52.** Concatenation of lattice channels (thesis p. 96). If $T_1$ and $T_2$ are lattice channels on the chain with $n$ modes per site, with rules $L_1$, $L_2$ and noise cells $\nu_1$, $\nu_2$, then $T_2 \circ T_1$ is a lattice channel with rule $L_2 L_1$ and noise cells $\nu_1 + L_{1*}\nu_2$. For all symbols $L$, $L_1$, $L_2$ and families $\nu$, $\nu_1$, $\nu_2$ of real $2n \times 2n$ matrices: the form of $L_*\nu$ is $\nu(L\cdot, L\cdot)$, with $\nu$ also denoting the form of the family; $N_{L_2 L_1} \subseteq N_{L_1} + N_{L_2}$; if $\nu_x = 0$ outside a finite set $A$, then $(L_*\nu)_d = 0$ for $d \notin A + (N_L - N_L)$; the range of $L^{\star T}\Omega L$ is contained in $N_L - N_L$; if $N_1$, $N_2$ are finite sets, $N_2$ nonempty, $N_{L_i} \subseteq N_i$ and $\nu_{i, x} = 0$ for $x \notin N_i - N_i$ ($i = 1, 2$), then $N_{L_2 L_1} \subseteq N_1 + N_2$ and $\nu_1 + L_{1*}\nu_2$ vanishes outside $(N_1 + N_2) - (N_1 + N_2)$; and for on-site cells $\delta_{\cdot, 0} a$, $\delta_{\cdot, 0} b$ of real matrices $a$, $b$, the cells $\delta_{\cdot, 0} a + L_{1*}(\delta_{\cdot, 0} b)$ vanish off the origin exactly when $L_{1*}(\delta_{\cdot, 0} b)$ does (4.39). The thesis writes the ranges of the composite as equalities; the statement gives the inclusions.  
<sub>`Sealed.dyn_concatenation`</sub>

**53.** The example system with noise: two steps with on-site noise (thesis p. 97, corrected) and a lossy chain. Let $r = (\sin\varphi, \cos\varphi)^T$, and for a real $2 \times 2$ matrix $\nu_0$ let $\delta_{\cdot, 0}\nu_0$ be the on-site cells. (a) The pull-back cell at distance two is $(L_{\varphi, \lambda *}\delta_{\cdot, 0}\nu_0)_2 = (\nu_0)_{qq}\, \lambda^2\, r r^T$. (b) If $\nu_0$ is positive semidefinite, $(L_{\varphi, \lambda *}\delta_{\cdot, 0}\nu_0)_x = 0$ for every $x \neq 0$ exactly when $\lambda = 0$ or $(\nu_0)_{qq} = 0$. By (4.39) these pull-back cells decide whether the noise of two steps with on-site noise $\nu_0$ stays on site, and the claim of the thesis that for nonzero coupling it does not holds for isotropic noise and fails for $\operatorname{diag}(0, b)$: (c) for $\lambda \neq 0$ and $c > 0$ the cell at distance two for $\nu_0 = c\, 1$ is nonzero; (d) for $\nu_0 = \operatorname{diag}(0, b)$ with $b \ge 0$ the pull-back cells vanish off the origin, for every $\lambda$. (e) The lossy chain: for $0 < \eta < 1$, the rule $\sqrt\eta\, L_{\varphi, \lambda}$ and the on-site noise cells $\delta_{\cdot, 0}(1 - \eta) 1$ satisfy the channel condition $\nu + i(\sigma - \sigma(\sqrt\eta L\cdot, \sqrt\eta L\cdot)) \ge 0$; the range of $(\sqrt\eta L)^{\star T}\Omega(\sqrt\eta L)$ is contained in $\{0\}$; the rule $\sqrt\eta\, L_{\varphi, \lambda}$ is not symplectic; and for $\lambda \neq 0$ the noise cells $\nu + (\sqrt\eta L)_*\nu$ of two steps are nonzero at distance $2$.  
<sub>`Sealed.dyn_example_noise`</sub>

**54.** The Fourier form of the channel condition (thesis (4.34)) for noise of finite range. Let $M$ be a symbol on the chain with $n$ modes per site and $(\nu_x)$ cell data of finite range, $\nu_m = 0$ for $|m| > R$. Then the rule $M$ and the noise $\nu$ (the form of the cells) satisfy the channel condition $\nu + i(\sigma - \sigma(M\cdot, M\cdot)) \ge 0$ exactly when the matrix $\hat\nu(k) + i\Omega - i\, \hat M(k)^\dagger \Omega \hat M(k)$ is positive semidefinite for every real $k$, where $\hat M(k)$ is the Fourier symbol of $M$ and ${}^\dagger$ the conjugate transpose.

**55.** Dilations on the chain at the level of covariances (thesis Lemma 4.12, with (4.40)). Let $S$ be a matrix of Laurent polynomials for $n + m$ modes per site, the $n$ system modes first, with $S^{\star T}(\Omega_n \oplus \Omega_m) S = \Omega_n \oplus \Omega_m$; let $S_{11}$ be its system-to-system block and $S_{21}$ its system-to-ancilla block (rows of the ancilla, columns of the system), and let $\gamma_0$ be a real $2m \times 2m$ matrix with $\gamma_0 + i\Omega_m \ge 0$.
